## 3. Official website evidence

- **Author:** University of Greenwich
- **Title:** [New AI guidance to support learning and assessments in 2026/27](https://www.gre.ac.uk/articles/public-relations/new-ai-guidance-to-support-learning-and-assessments-in-202627)
- **Published:** 9 September 2026
- **Accessed:** 8 October 2026
- **Verification:** Public article inspected; this repository stores a source summary, not an archived copy of the webpage or complete policy.

The article describes three assessment tiers:

| Tier | Published meaning, summarised |
|---|---|
| 1 — Independent of AI | Restricted use to demonstrate the student's own knowledge, skills or judgement. |
| 2 — Supported by AI | Support for planning, clarification, feedback and refinement; the student retains responsibility. |
| 3 — Augmented with AI | Generative AI is intentionally incorporated into the assessment and critically evaluated. |

Staff determine the tier and assessment briefs specify permitted uses. The 2026/27 update calls for an AI Reflection (AIR), describing AI's contribution, the student's review/revisions and their own contribution, instead of the previously required declaration form. Students remain responsible for accuracy and originality; fabricated evidence is unacceptable. These statements are supported by the [published article](https://www.gre.ac.uk/articles/public-relations/new-ai-guidance-to-support-learning-and-assessments-in-202627), not by an independently inspected full policy.

The article links to [Responsible use of generative AI in teaching and learning](https://www.gre.ac.uk/docs/rep/information-and-library-services/responsible-use-of-generative-ai-in-teaching-and-learning). The full linked document could not be accessed during the October website review. That is a historical access limitation, distinct from the student AI and referencing PDFs supplied and inspected on 5 November. Their current requirements are recorded below; no new live-website inspection is claimed.

## 4. Applicability to COMP1884

**Confirmed by Chathuranga — 5 November 2026:** In the working conversation, Chathuranga confirmed that COMP1884 continues under **Tier 2 — Supported by AI**, and instructed this repository to follow the newly supplied student guidance. This supersedes the earlier “suggested Tier 2 / tier unconfirmed” wording. It records his confirmation without inventing a separate lecturer statement.

Under the supplied Tier 2 guidance, AI supports learning, planning, feedback, grammar, clarification and refinement. Students retain responsibility for argument, evidence, analysis, interpretation and final work. Acknowledgement does not turn generated writing into the student’s own original work or verify generated references/results.

The supplied student guidance requires an **AI Reflection (AIR)** for Tier 2/3 work, placed at the end of the assignment **before the references**. Use this current requirement for repository planning. The earlier declaration form and report/essay template references remain historical source context; they are no longer an unresolved choice blocking this update.

| Submission | Current guidance to carry into preparation |
|---|---|
| Group report | Record the actual AI assistance and human contribution; include the AIR before references in the final document. |
| Individual reflective essay | Preserve individual authorship and factual experience; include the AIR before references. The AI-use statement is distinct from the required reflection on project delivery/teamwork. |
| Individual presentation and questions | The presentation specification requires acknowledgement of AI assistance; the general student guidance requires an AIR before references. It does not prescribe a slide-specific AIR layout. Apply those requirements without presenting a particular slide format as a quoted university rule. Delivery and answers must demonstrate the student’s own understanding. |

The guidance offers **CRITIC** to structure an AIR:

| Element | Account to record |
|---|---|
| Context | Tier 2 and the task being completed. |
| Role | Tool used and the role it played. |
| Interrogate | How accuracy, bias, evidence, relevance and limitations were checked. |
| Transform | What was accepted, rejected, revised or developed further, and why. |
| Impact | How the assistance affected the approach and learning. |
| Contribution | The student’s own intellectual, practical or creative contribution. |

These are documentation requirements, not a completed AIR or a new research decision. Actual submission statements will use the members’ real experiences; no retrospective activity or approval is invented. The [criteria source register](greenwich-criteria-checklist.md#sources) and [reference register](references.md#university--project-guidance) identify the new sources.

[AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) and [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) require human review of material work and separate authorisation for consequential experiments and Git operations. Those repository controls remain in force; they do not replace university permission.

## 5. Recording actual AI assistance

For each member's relevant use, retain a concise factual account of the tool, task, affected work, output accepted or rejected, verification/revisions, and the member's own contribution. Link existing Issues, reviewed diffs, source checks and experiment records where useful. Record only use and review that actually happened; do not invent retrospective logs or claim generated text/code was entirely written by the student.

Use that account to prepare the AIR required by the supplied guidance. This note is not a completed submission statement; the factual account remains subject to human review.

The supplied GenAI referencing guide covers generated text, images, code and ideas. For Harvard citations to prompted chat content it gives a company/year in-text citation and a reference containing company, year, medium/tool, prompt, generation date, chat URL and access date. Use actual details and verify original scholarly references independently; do not copy the guide’s example dates as project facts. The guide advises checking module instructions about conversation appendices; it does not impose a universal requirement to publish every chat. Preserve private information when preparing any permitted reference or appendix.
